Because on the retail side, the "egress" firewall is also the front door
for the SD-WAN overlay. A store whose WAN transport is the internet,
whether broadband or a T-Mobile cellular link, can only reach the 128T
head-ends by coming in through the internet edge, and the RSIA/DMVPN
firewall tier is what sits on that edge.

The path, from the retail topology skills:

The head-end conductors at RI are NAT'd 1:1 from 10.230.125.196–.203 to
public 12.230.70.242–.253, which AT&T AS 2386 advertises. There are also
four statics, 207.242.146.201–.204/32, pointed at the RSIA firewall. At
AZ (Shea), the conductors NAT from 10.231.125.196–.203 to
12.51.52.18–.29, with statics 12.28.145.9–.12/32 pointed at the DMVPN
firewall. That's from the 2020 drawing, so verify it's still current. So
a store on T-Mobile goes: T-Mobile → internet → AT&T/COX/Lumen edge
routers → RRICMDC-SEC-EXTRSIA-01/02 or the rricmcpsecextrs-1/DMVPN
context → head-end. The firewall named for retail store egress is the
same device that terminates this inbound overlay traffic, on its
outside-to-inside path.

Why it reads as "egress" in your view:

The device is named and classed by its main job. In Firewall Class terms
it supports both Internet Egress and Internet Ingress. The overlay
transport is ingress through it, even though most of its other traffic
is store users going out. CCD has no specific classification for the
head-end NAT space. Per the topology skill, 12.230.70.x and 12.51.52.x
are only covered by a broad 12.0.0.0/8 catch-all rule object in
external_ip_registry (CVS_OWNED=N). Any report built on the EIR
therefore sees "T-Mobile → AT&T 12.x" as generic external traffic
crossing an internet-edge firewall. Nothing tells it this is CVS's own
SD-WAN head-end. That's a real enrichment gap worth fixing regardless of
how this event turns out.

The quick test: look at the destination IP of the burst.

If it's one of the head-end addresses above (or the current-equivalent
NAT after the COX/Lumen 152.145.240.0/24 cutover), this is store SD-WAN
transport arriving through the edge firewall, exactly as designed. If
it's anything else (a 204.99/198.187 VIP, an RSIA NAT address used for
store browsing, or random addresses), the SD-WAN explanation falls apart
and the flood reading comes back into play.
